Eversince I started college, I was in need of a note-taking app and since I am a customization-freak, obsidian stood out as the most flexible option, allowing me to add plugins and truly ‘personalize’ my note-taking experience. And as a student I would prefer to have my notes available both on my phone and on my laptop.

Two slight issues arise here. First thing that came to my mind was to check if Obsidian has its own built-in sync option and it has Obsidian Sync. However, since I’m a broke student and don’t want to pay for anything if not necessary, I decided to check if there were some free alternatives.

Syncthing

Open source, encrypted and decentralized. Syncthing allows users to sync folders between multiple devices. I used Syncthing previously to automatically upload pictures from my previous Pixel phone to my laptop. After I switched to an iPhone, I came to know that Apple restricts background use for apps much more strictly than Android. When I was researching ways to sync Obsidian, I found lukm’s guide on using syncthing specifically for Obsidian. This guide is inspired by this.

Synctrain

For iOS, Synctrain is a modern syncthing client which most importantly has options for creating shortcuts & automations via iOS’s Shortcuts app.

Shortcut & automation

  • Sync Automation -> self-explainable; every time I open or exit Obsidian on my phone, the directory is scanned for changes and synced with my Laptop (if available)
  • Pull shortcut -> Since constant background syncthing usage would not work (I think at least?) I use this shortcut to simply pull any changes I made on my laptop
